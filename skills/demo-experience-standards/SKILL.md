# Shared demo experience standards

Use these decisions for newly authored visual experiences across customer demo sites, presentations and Voice Demo Builder. Preserve each customer's identity and the existing authoring/runtime owner. This skill supplies experience guidance, not a renderer, deployment workflow or AVA executor.

Read [accepted decisions and lessons](references/decisions.md) when choosing motion, reusable components or 3D assets, or when a user correction should improve future work.

For the core CSS/21st.dev/GSAP/Blender/Three.js stack, use the [implementation playbook](references/implementation-playbook.md). Start from the audience task and select only the layers the output needs.

When selecting the next visual technology, use the dated [technology candidates](references/technology-options.md) as a starting point and verify current source, licence and runtime fit. They are research candidates, not adopted dependencies.

## Choosing motion and interaction

When a brief would benefit from a visual treatment, use the [motion selection guide](references/motion-selection.md). Start with the audience benefit and still alternative, prefer the target's existing native pattern, and distinguish available Atlas components from review pilots and research. Choose still content for dense evidence and task-critical copy; never use animation to establish real activity or readiness. Carry the selected treatment or deliberate omission into the existing specialist handoff. This is selection guidance, not a request to apply effects everywhere or upgrade a renderer.

## Accepted presentation standard

Simon approved the Co-op visual storytelling, client | IPI cover and matching character treatment as the standard on 22 November 2026. For new customer-service presentations, introduce both the customer and the agent, each with a goal and friction. Later, connect the agent’s frustrations to specific supported capabilities and show how the work changes, while keeping their judgement and authority clear. Keep stage copy light, use coherent imagery, and retain full reading explanations and private presenter detail. Give every client a distinct art direction; the standard does not require Co-op’s people, styling or all five technologies. Presentation-authoring owns the versioned runtime and template.

## Human story check

Read each customer presentation without speaker notes before delivery. The audience should be able to follow the person’s need, the help, the action and a concrete resolution. Carry that outcome into later confirmations, summaries and the ending. Use ordinary dialogue and specific people, objects and actions. Remove AI-like explanation and generic governance/implementation footnotes when they interrupt the story; retain a material condition if the audience needs it to understand an actual claim. Keep natural customer imagery and avoid forced emotion. Agent imagery should establish employer, role and a believable workplace through clothing, branding and equipment, while preserving the deck’s art direction. Keep authored outcomes, generated settings and capability assumptions private; they are not observed events or actual employee photographs.

## Features that help the people

When a customer-service deck explains a product, connect selected supported features to what the agent does and how that helps the customer. A concise feature → agent → customer map may introduce later scenes that show the help in use; it need not become an exhaustive catalogue. Keep the person reviewing suggestions and making decisions. Verify capabilities against primary sources and keep configuration assumptions private.

When performance figures strengthen the story, use named, attributable primary evidence. Keep the metric, unit, range or “up to”, customer scope and material contributing work visible. Link the case study. Distinguish another customer's reported result from local measurements or a forecast; never sum unlike metrics or attribute a wider platform result to one feature. Keep detailed source review and exclusions in operator material. Keep numbers static by default. When emphasis is requested or useful, a short count-up can play once on first stage entry. Preserve final accessible/static values and all qualifiers; settle on interruption and keep reading, print and reduced-motion output still.

For Copilot evidence, use the versioned presentation-authoring skill's `references/copilot-evidence-bank.md` and structured bank. Choose the customer's first value and demonstrated capability before selecting a figure. Match audience, task, industry and source scope; use recent repetition as a final tie-breaker. Usually choose one primary proof and one useful complement from different organisations. Keep exact qualifiers and combined-programme attribution. Never default to the same pair or substitute a larger but less relevant number. Recheck sources per deck and save selected IDs, bank revision, date and rationale privately. Do not put sales proof onto a customer task screen unless it helps that task.

## Short moments and a clear next step

A brief presenter-controlled walkthrough can show a capability in use within the deck’s own visual language. Keep native progression, reversible state, complete reading/static content and reduced motion. Use a verified recording excerpt when it matches the story and remains legible; retain recorded UI and theme its surrounding frame. Preserve the existing full-length recording/player workflow. Authored illustration and actual capture have different provenance; keep that distinction accurate privately.

Give repeated scenes different jobs, move optional exploration into backup when it interrupts the story, and close with a concrete request type, team, baseline and measures. Preserve chosen partnership branding through custom scenes, including contrast on dark slides and clear mobile headers.

## Full-demo handoff

When full demonstrations are part of the presentation, establish the people, friction and relevant help before handing over to the selected native players. Return to what the chosen example shows, then attributable results and a focused next decision. Make the entry, choice of another example and return to the story clear. Keep the player's media, audio, timing, native controls and download workflow intact; theme only its surrounding frame. A short illustrated moment can support the story but does not replace the full-demo workflow. Do not infer a completed transaction, transfer or audience viewing from opening a recording.

Use subtle physical depth and press feedback selectively on prominent actions when it fits the brand. Keep navigation quiet, keyboard focus clear, disabled treatment flat and reduced motion still. The Holcim demo launch buttons are a bounded trial, not a portfolio-wide style change. Simon has explicitly chosen this treatment for Voice Demo Builder primary actions on mobile and desktop; retain its compact-on-scroll mobile identity header and task controls. See the dated decision record for scope.

## Apply the lessons

- Decorative motion plays naturally, settles promptly and has no replay or watch-again button. Preserve purposeful playback, slide navigation, guided explanation and real task controls.
- For a blank-canvas presentation brief, establish a new narrative and art direction before selecting layouts or effects. Give typography, imagery, scale and pacing a reason in the story; retain the existing source, navigation and private-output contracts.
- Select animation to explain a relationship, guide attention or give interaction feedback. Keep a quiet/static treatment when it serves the task. A new client does not inherit Co-op's grocery artwork or visual style.
- Presentation covers should blend the retained client and IPI marks with a visible pipe. For character-led stories, introduce the customer before the friction and the receiving colleague at the handoff. Match generated portraits to the deck’s palette, lighting and composition; return to their needs as the story resolves. Preserve generation provenance privately.
- Match the explanation to the audience. A simulated customer website should help customers complete their task. Keep customer/AVA/knowledge/adviser architecture explanations in the kit bag or presenter-led material unless the customer brief explicitly needs them. The same component may be adapted to a useful customer process with appropriate content and truthful state.
- Prefer CSS/SVG and native browser animation for small static pages. Adapt selected 21st.dev components to the current brand and framework after reviewing their source, dependencies and licence. Do not add a framework solely for a small effect.
- Treat 21st.dev as an optional reference when a new component or improvement would help. Discover suitable examples without requiring Simon to name one. If nothing fits or its source cannot be reviewed, continue with an appropriate custom/native solution; a library search must not hold up delivery.
- Keep paid or substantial-runtime tools as bounded trials until their benefit justifies licensing, asset production and maintenance. Preserve useful sources in the kit bag when a trial is reserved; do not turn an experiment into a portfolio default.
- Use Blender where custom depth, lighting or materials earn the production work. Prefer an optimised render for decoration. Add live 3D when visitor interaction benefits from it and performance has been checked.
- Respect reduced-motion changes, retain keyboard focus and keep the content complete when animation or JavaScript is unavailable. Presentations also need complete print/static compositions.
- Keep factual progress and readiness independent from decorative animation. In Voice Demo Builder, a completed visual sequence cannot expose a ready/call action before the existing verified readiness gates pass.

## Keep learning

Record an actual user correction or observed result as a dated decision, including its scope, rationale, example and checks. Separate accepted decisions from ideas awaiting a prototype. Carry the applicable decision into new briefs and specialist handoffs without asking the user to repeat it. Promote a tested pattern, not an entire client's styling.

Keep exact sources and validation limits in operator material. Update this source skill, its installed copy and the portfolio catalogue when its scope or ownership changes. Existing packages and native runtimes require their normal release process; a guidance update is not a runtime upgrade.

“Voice Demo Builder” is the working audience-facing name for the unattended website-to-voice workflow. Keep existing `website-receptionist` APIs, saved identities and Environment Builder/official AVA ownership unchanged. Do not rename existing native resources as a side effect.
